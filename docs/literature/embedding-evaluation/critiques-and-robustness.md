# Critiques of embedding evaluation: leaderboard overfitting, static-benchmark saturation, instruction and paraphrase sensitivity, representational limits

- **Kind:** methodology note (critiques and the evaluation designs proposed in response)
- **Links:** AIR-Bench [arXiv:2412.13102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13102); PTEB [arXiv:2510.06730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06730); FollowIR [arXiv:2403.15246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15246); InstructIR [arXiv:2402.14334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14334); Promptriever [arXiv:2409.11136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11136); LIMIT [arXiv:2508.21038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21038); BRIGHT [arXiv:2407.12883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12883); NV-Embed [arXiv:2405.17428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17428); Gemini Embedding [arXiv:2503.07891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07891)
- **Authors / org, date:** 2024–2026 (see links)
- **Status:** MTEB's own reforms (the zero-shot score, the MMTEB task selection, `MTEB(eng, v2)` dropping MS MARCO/NQ, the move to verified results, RTEB private sets and the RTEB governance incident) are covered in [mteb.md](mteb.md) and are **not repeated here**. This note adds the other critiques and the evaluation designs that answer them.

## What it is

Five failure modes of standard embedding evaluation, each paired with a countermeasure from the literature:

| Failure mode | Countermeasure in the literature |
|---|---|
| Leaderboard overfitting through in-domain training data | Held-out or closed test sets (AIR-Bench, RTEB), zero-shot disclosure (MTEB) |
| Static test beds that saturate or get memorized | Evaluation-time regeneration (PTEB), dynamic versions (AIR-Bench) |
| Instruction and prompt sensitivity | Paired-instruction metrics (FollowIR p-MRR), worst-case metrics (InstructIR Robustness@k) |
| Paraphrase and lexical sensitivity | Stochastic paraphrasing (PTEB), lexical-overlap filtering of queries (BRIGHT, LitSearch; see [domain-benchmarks.md](domain-benchmarks.md)) |
| Representational limits of single vectors | Stress tests designed from theory (LIMIT) |

## How it works

### 1. Overfitting and the case for held-out, generated test sets

- **Model authors say it themselves.** Gemini Embedding's related-work section says earlier generalist models' "reliance on extensive in-domain training datasets has resulted in overfitting to specific benchmarks" ([Gemini, related work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07891)).
- **Rank reversals between public and held-out sets.** NV-Embed reports that SFR-Embedding-2R beats SFR-Embedding-Mistral on MTEB (70.31 vs 67.56) but loses on the closed AIR-Bench (QA 49.47 vs 51.58; Long-Doc 67.45 vs 69.0) ([NV-Embed App. AIR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17428)). One pair is an anecdote, not a statistic, but it shows why a held-out set is needed.
- **AIR-Bench design** ([arXiv:2412.13102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13102)):
  - Queries, hard negatives and relevance labels are generated by LLMs from real corpora, in three stages: corpus preparation, candidate generation, and quality control. Quality control has an LLM filter for low-quality queries and a three-step correction of false relevance labels.
  - The authors argue that generated data "is almost impossible to be covered by the training sets of any existing retrievers."
  - Test ground truth is withheld, and scores come only through the HF Hub platform ([NV-Embed's description](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17428)).
  - It is versioned (24.04, 24.05, …) as a "Dynamic" benchmark.
- **How AIR-Bench validated generated labels.** This is the part most worth copying:
  1. Regenerate MS MARCO (G-MSMARCO) from the same positives.
  2. Score 17 retrievers on both the real and the generated version with nDCG@10.
  3. Compute the **Spearman rank correlation of the system ordering**: 0.8211 (p=5e-5).
  4. Ablate quality control: 0.6912 without it.
  5. Resample 2,000-query subsets 30 times: mean 0.8031.

  Absolute nDCG was higher on the generated set for every model. The authors attribute this to quality control finding more positives. So **generated labels can preserve rankings without preserving absolute scores** ([AIR-Bench, Consistency Analysis (RQ1)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13102)).
- **Testing leakage directly (BRIGHT).** BRIGHT continued training GritLM on all of the StackExchange documents in its pool, with a language-modeling loss plus contrastive training on QA pairs, to simulate pretraining exposure. It then checked whether scores went up. The average nDCG@10 over the 7 StackExchange sets went from 20.5 to 20.4, which the authors read as the benchmark being robust to document exposure because the query-to-document mapping needs reasoning ([BRIGHT, "Robustness against data leakage from pretraining", Table "robust to massive pre-training"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12883)). The per-domain changes are large and go both ways, for example Psychology 19.9 → 30.7 and Earth Science 32.8 → 25.5. An unchanged average can therefore hide real per-domain leakage effects. This is an **active contamination test**, not an overlap audit.

### 2. Static test beds and paraphrase sensitivity

- **PTEB** argues that "repeated tuning on a fixed suite can inflate reported scores and obscure real-world robustness". It generates meaning-preserving paraphrases at evaluation time with an LLM, validates them against gold ratings and human checks, and aggregates over multiple stochastic runs ([PTEB abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06730)). Across 7 MTEB tasks, 20 datasets and 25 languages, performance "is sensitive to changes in token space even when semantics remain fixed", and smaller models "are not disproportionately affected relative to larger ones".
- **What we take from it:** evaluate over **distributions of phrasings**, and report the mean and the spread across runs, not one fixed string per information need.

### 3. Instruction sensitivity

- **FollowIR** reuses TREC narratives (the instructions given to assessors) as retrieval instructions, then alters them so that some previously relevant documents become non-relevant. Instruction-following is measured with **p-MRR**, a pairwise rank-change metric on a −100 to 100 scale, alongside standard MAP or nDCG@5. The finding: existing retrievers "fail to correctly use instructions, using them for basic keywords" ([FollowIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15246)).
- **InstructIR** writes user-aligned instructions for each query and introduces **Robustness@k**: the minimum nDCG@k over the instructions attached to the same query, averaged over queries. It found that task-style instruction-tuned retrievers (INSTRUCTOR) "can underperform compared to their non-instruction-tuned counterparts", and that INSTRUCTOR's robustness *falls* as model size grows ([InstructIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14334)). Lexical-overlap analysis showed that "relying heavily on lexical cues can lead to wrong targets" (same paper, analysis section).
- **Promptriever** reports that instruction-trained retrievers gain +12.9 Robustness@10 on InstructIR and +14.3 p-MRR on FollowIR. It also shows prompting as "hyperparameter search" giving +1.4 on BEIR ([Promptriever abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11136)). That second result means **prompt search is a tunable knob, and tuning it on the test set leaks**.
- **Prompt choice matters in model papers too.** Configuration-level effects are sizable, for example E5-mistral at 64.5 vs 60.3 MTEB average with and without natural-language instructions ([reporting-protocols.md](reporting-protocols.md)). Reproduction failures from missing prefixes are documented in [mteb.md](mteb.md).

### 4. Representational limits (LIMIT)

- **The theory.** LIMIT connects the embedding dimension to how many distinct top-k sets can be returned: "for a given embedding dimension d there exist top-k combinations of documents that cannot be returned — no matter the query" ([LIMIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21038)).
- **The dataset** is built from that theory. It has 50k documents and 1000 queries, each with exactly 2 relevant documents, attribute-style ("who likes Apples?"). There is also a 46-document version in which every pair is a query.
- **Findings:**
  - State-of-the-art embedders "struggle to reach even 20% recall@100" on the full set and cannot solve the 46-document version even at recall@20.
  - BM25 does "very well due to their higher dimensionality".
  - Performance rises with dimension (the dimension sweeps mark MRL-trained models).
- **Relevance for Lean.** Queries like "lemmas that are both `@[simp]` and about `Finset.card`", or "the monotonicity lemma for X in ordered fields", combine attributes. LIMIT suggests a single-vector first stage may have a hard ceiling on such combinatorial relevance that no training fixes. A benchmark should include such queries, in a separate category, so this limit is visible rather than averaged away.

### 5. Domain mismatch

General-benchmark rank predicts domain rank only moderately. MTEB-BR reports Spearman 0.75, and SABER-Math reports poor prediction of math performance; both are summarized in [mteb.md](mteb.md). Math- and Lean-specific evidence is in [../lean-benchmarks/mirb.md](../lean-benchmarks/mirb.md), [../math-ir/saber-math.md](../math-ir/saber-math.md) and [../lean-benchmarks/legendre-leaderboard.md](../lean-benchmarks/legendre-leaderboard.md). The methodological consequence: **a Lean benchmark cannot borrow an embedder ordering from MTEB; it must measure its own.**

## Evaluation (limits of the countermeasures)

- **Generated test sets inherit the generator's biases.** AIR-Bench validated ranking agreement on MS MARCO only, a web-QA domain where LLMs are strong. Agreement on Lean, where LLM informalizations are themselves an engine component, is unknown.
- **Closed test sets move trust to whoever holds them.** This is the RTEB lesson in [mteb.md](mteb.md).
- **Robustness@k (a minimum) is noisy with few variants per group** and heavily penalizes one bad paraphrase. Report it next to the mean, not instead of it.

## Relevance to lean-explore-bench

1. **Held-out split with rotation.** Keep a private query split (queries plus qrels), publish its construction procedure and summary statistics, and evaluate submissions only through our harness. Rotate or extend it with each Mathlib version, following AIR-Bench's versioning.
2. **Validate any LLM-generated queries or labels the AIR-Bench way.** Build a small human-labeled Lean set, generate a parallel LLM version from the same targets, and report the Spearman correlation of system rankings, with and without our quality-control step. Only scale the generated set if the correlation is high.
3. **Paraphrase groups.** For a subset of targets, create 3–5 phrasings: informal English, LaTeX-ish, Lean-syntax fragment, name guess, and a "de-lexicalized" version with low name overlap. Report mean and Robustness@10.
4. **Instruction protocol.** Fix instructions ahead of time. Report "no instruction" and "model-card instruction" runs. Do not search prompts on the test split. If prompt search is allowed, do it on a dev split and report it as a separate configuration.
5. **Active contamination probes.** For engines that fine-tune on Mathlib-derived data, re-score on a subset whose targets were added to Mathlib after the engine's data snapshot (a temporal split). The BRIGHT-style exposure test is the model-side analogue.
6. **A combinatorial-query category**, reported separately (LIMIT-style), so that first-stage single-vector limits show up.

## Open questions

- Can a temporal split (declarations added after a cutoff) serve as our "private" set without the governance problems of a closed set? Its queries could be fully public while still unseen by earlier-trained models.
- How many paraphrases per target are needed before Robustness@10 is stable enough to rank systems?
